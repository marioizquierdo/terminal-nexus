# 2026-11-11 — Single battles: Skirmish at the centre, Story for the campaign

The owner, after the kept front and the banner prototype merged, asked what the modes should be. The research he asked
for is [single battles](../reports/2026-11-11-single-battles.md); the decision is the third note.

### F244

> Let's check the general status of the game design before we move into next milestone. I think currently we are working on the run mode, but I am having serious doubts about what this mode actually looks like in practice. See, playing a game, unlocking any unit, playing another game, unlocking any unit. That sounds fun in theory, but this game has long rounds. Even if each round is 30 seconds, the full game may be 30 minutes, which means we don't really have a lot of leeway to make a full round. While a game like Slay the Spire has quick battles and it quickly moves into the next part of building the deck, and there's different ways, and it's very much designed around quick battles. Other auto battlers that do this also have a very quick battle setup. I think we should focus more and lean into the idea of a real-time strategy game, very much like a StarCraft, but with a very distinctive gameplay based on building, preparing, placing a banner, deciding what the army is supposed to do overall, and then watch how the strategy unveils for the next round. After that, deciding what should be the technology that is developed on next round, while the enemy is doing the same, which means the player is taking a bet and a guess of what the enemy is choosing, and is not able to see what they chose until the next round, including a new banner placement. So that comes as a surprise and hopefully an interesting battle round, collecting resources and technology development for the next round. So based on that, I think the game should really lean into single battles. The obvious mode is the quick play, which I think we should develop more in detail as well. I think we're currently calling versus, but let me know what terminology we should be using here, in which you can customize the rules of the battle. I imagine something similar to StarCraft or Age of Empires quick game, in which you have an enemy or a couple of enemies, but let's simplify to just having one enemy. You choose which enemy is commander is fighting against you, which commander you are fighting, and then perhaps we can have a few rules. Maybe you can customize the difficulty, choose the map, and maybe a different starting point. But I think we will figure out as we go. What I like about developing this versus or quick mode is that it really helps for testing. So this mode should allow to set up any sort of demo to try out different aspects of the game with different commanders, and even have experimental way to set up the grid with already starting units. If not through the UI, at the very least, allow you to start a new game with this type of setup, maybe by creating a configuration file and pointing at it before the quick game begins. I think that's basically what we are already doing for testing, but we could formalize it into a game mode that actually is playable, so we can start playing the game ourselves as soon as possible. The campaign mode still stays relevant as a tutorial, and there is a lot of great ideas like intros and ways to set up puzzles that help the player discover how new units can be used. And obviously, a little bit of narrative really builds up into the lore of the game for each commander. That seems quite fun. It can have levels that progressively resolve after each other, and also a series of smaller achievements or secondary goals that people can pursue if they want to replay the campaign and do it in a more difficult setting by trying to define certain achievements that are harder or require deeper strategy, or even require unlocking certain things in certain order. All of that seems fun, but I believe for us to create an actual gameplay that requires human player choice and strategy, we need to start focusing in creating a play loop that we can set up and try and evaluate to see how fun it is. At the same time, we polish and improve the UI by playing and finding out what things make it more intuitive versus things that make it more cumbersome. So yes, basically I think the run mode and the campaign should merge into one. It will be more fun if the campaign allows certain areas where you can perhaps branch the campaign into multiple directions by making different technological choices, while keeping a united single narrative of the events that are happening for each nexus. So I'd like you to research a little bit of this new direction, contrast versus our pending plans before. Research if there is any other games working on this, and looking at the list of games that we already identify, and think about the gameplay duration and what that really means for the different play modes, and what benefit we have from each one of them versus how difficult it could be to develop each one of them. After running with a few ideas, please write it down in a summary that I can listen to and think about for the next iteration.

**Built** as research: [single battles](../reports/2026-11-11-single-battles.md).

### F245

> Yes, I like the idea of a skirmish campaign. That is the model we should keep. However, I still want to explore the idea of a run, but in a different way, not through multiple battles, but through a single battle. The thing I'm building in my head is the idea of an endless wave of enemies where the player keeps building and is trying to hit a high score of how far was able to make it. And every time they do that, they unlock a new theme that they can use on the next run. So I am well aware this is basically a roguelike mode, and that could be the whole game, not just a mode inside the game. And actually building this properly takes a lot of balancing. So this could be incompatible or slow down the other modes. Whether like campaign is more fun and skirmish is more flexible, and the future possible PvP mode will basically build on top of skirmish. By having a skirmish, we allows us to find the settings that are more enjoyable in most cases for typical PvP battle. So skirmish seems to be the cornerstone of how we play this game, and the thing that we should honestly focus on first until we can have an enjoyable skirmish experience. But yeah, before we settle into these ideas, I would like to explore an endless mode where users aim for a high score, or perhaps that can be built in inside the campaign. Maybe campaigns should have an endless level that tests how well they did on the previous levels of the campaign. So they could replay it, trying to unlock better units or better upgrades. So in the final endless level, they can hit a high score. However, that is not going to be a satisfactory ending of the campaign. The campaign should end up with an epic final victory that perhaps could come after the skirmish mode. Perhaps that final victory happens after your nexus is compromised on the skirmish mode. Maybe one last chance to defeat the enemy. So that seems like a reasonable order for campaign, and it will be interesting to organize all of the campaigns in the same way. And at that moment, perhaps campaign is not the best name for this mode. It will be more like adventure or structure or commander. Story mode would be, and it will start with a tutorial game, then move with a second, third, fourth, and fifth games where the focus is not only passing the map, but unlocking some technologies or achievements. And depending on what you do, you accumulate new technologies for later. And all of that builds into a final endless wave where the player needs to balance collecting resources with proper upgrades, with holding on the enemy, with taking into strategical points, and basically hit the highest score possible up to the point that their nexus is taken. And at that moment, it unlocks the final scene that jumps into the final level, which is basically trying to defeat one of the nexuses, awaken Eternals, and that will be basically the final boss battle for that commander. Yeah, let me know if this structure makes sense, if you find it interesting, and if you can crystallize a more structured path to slowly build something that is enjoyable, or if we are getting complicated here. What do you think?

**Built** as a proposal in the conversation: three kinds of battle on one setup (a Skirmish, a scripted mission, an
endless stand), a Commander's story ending in the endless stand and a last-chance final battle, and a path one milestone
at a time.

**Built.** The modes are Skirmish, Story and Versus ([the game modes](../../game-design/game-modes.md)). Skirmish is the
centre of design and testing: one battle against a scripted Commander Army, set up on a screen or by a battle file. A
mission exists only inside Story, where a Commander's story runs from a tutorial through technology missions to an
endless stand and a final battle ([Story](../../game-design/story.md)); any mission still opens directly for testing.
Challenge runs are retired: their design is [kept in history](../challenge-runs-design.md) and the runs milestone is
closed. The Skirmish milestone is current, and takes the stance and the banner with it; the Story milestones follow.
