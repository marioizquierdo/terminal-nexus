### F211 — Double down on the RTS: the front stays, 30-second rounds, a regroup, and banners

> This is fantastic data. I am also leaning into double down the RTS style, leaving troops on the map, so they can
> keep moving forward. I think we can improve the troop movement to self-arrange in useful formations, have a better
> way to define aggressive vs defensive stance, clear movement and pathfinding algorithms, and predictable spawning
> schedules. With all that, we can safely leave the units on the map.
>
> Let's aim for this:
>
> * Shorter rounds: 30 seconds.
> * Keep units on the map after a fight. If they are in battle, they stop targeting, move back for 2 seconds, and
>   self-arrange in formation for 6 seconds (10 seconds total). This should happen during the build phase already.
> * It's ok to have troops moving during build phase, allow them to self arrange in formation, and move out when a
>   building is placed on top of them. But it's important that this is deterministic, so scripted battles stay
>   deterministic as well.
> * Aggressive vs Defensive stance: Yes I think this will be important. I want to formalize the idea of battle
>   banners. From simpler to complex:
>    * no banners, no stance (current): troops are always aggressive towards the enemy nexus. During campaign mode,
>      they have an automatic target (defined by the campaign) -- This is the simplest option but it doesn't leave
>      much room for strategic tactics.
>    * stance: a global setting to define aggressive, hold the line, or defensive stance -- Your initial suggestion,
>      this is simple enough, and already allows for a lot more dynamism.
>    * banner: a positional banner on the map, that can be placed as aggressive or defensive. By default, it is placed
>      on the enemy nexus as aggressive. If placed anywhere else on the map as aggressive the units move there first,
>      and then from there they keep going to the enemy nexus, which allows to define routes. Placed anywhere as
>      defensive, the units hold the line at that point, which allows for map control, or to defend your base.
>    * Commander and Secondary banners: two positional banners instead of one. The commander always follows the main
>      banner. Buildings can be selected to spawn units that follow the secondary banner instead. This allows for
>      complex tactics, which will be very important and interesting with resources and special bonuses that could be
>      held in the map. But it requires more UX details. I would like to eventually test this option, but we can
>      build incrementally towards this.

**Built** (the kept front; the stance and the banner are scheduled). The question is answered (the front stays) and the decisions are in
[`core-loop.md`](../../milestones/core-loop.md). The runs milestone gains three steps before the route: the kept front
(30-second rounds, the regroup played during the Build Phase, units stepping out from under a new building), the
stance, and the banner. Two banners are written down as the direction and wait for the economy and held map bonuses.

### F212 — One mechanism for every mode; the modes named; the regroup as an idea; placement pushes troops

> - Campaign missions: should use the same mechanism as p2p/versus and run/rogue modes, we are no longer sending troops
>   back to the origin base. We should cleanup that code. Don't worry about breaking the current levels, they are
>   experimental still and will evolve as we work on the other modes. The only thing that is different on
>   campaign/story levels is the enemies and cut-scenes. Enemies don't need a nexus, and can have more elaborate
>   scripting, but the gameplay should be the same. For now we can use defensive stand or aggressive automatically, but
>   later will have to update to use stances or banners throughout phase conditions (asking the user tonplace the
>   banner as a tutorial, etc).
> - lets formalize the game modes. I realize we have to be more precise about this, even during design: Campaign (is a
>   tutorial for each commander with intros, we should rename to Story mode on the next PR), Run (play a standard
>   progression of maps with upgrades in between, we should rename to Rogue mode), and p2p (nexus versus nexus, two
>   commanders vs each other with the competitive build unlocked, we should rename as Versus mode). Take note of the
>   rename, but we will formally address on the next PR.
> - regroup 10 seconds: please take this as a guideline/recommendation, I don't know what numbers will work well, we
>   are still trying to find the core loop that works. The idea is what matters right now. During a pulse pause, units
>   need to disengafe from battle, and this gives then a chance to re-group and re-target. Then 30 second timing will
>   become an essential skill for battle tactics, perhaps some units will survive thanks to the re-grouping. So it
>   should be subtle, perhaps just letting units to move backwards for 5 seconds is enough, because they should favor
>   formations towards the enemy when they move, or maybe move backwards 2 seconds and then a defensive self arrange
>   for 8 seconds as if there were no enemy units in sight. It has to be intuitive, thematic (imagine the Nexus
>   announces a pause and troops must reapect the will of the nexus, but they would still move and prepare for next
>   round) and deterministic before the next battle round.
> - placing buildings moving troops is something we needed anyways. We are now formalizing it so it works for friendly
>   and enemy troops, pathfinding, and it is deterministic while during gameplay the troop moves smoothly towards the
>   new position, which may push and re-arrange other troops in the area, looking really cool and useful at the same
>   time.

**Built.** Folded into the kept front: every mode keeps the front and the walk home is removed; a story level's side
takes its automatic target, or a defensive stand where a level needs one. The regroup's numbers are a starting point
chosen by what reads on screen. A building placed on troops, friendly or enemy, pushes them out along the ground, in a
ripple, the same way every time. The three modes' new names are noted in
[`core-loop.md`](../../milestones/core-loop.md) for the next pull request.

What now happens: a Battle Round lasts 30 seconds and a duel or run battle up to eight of them. When a round ends,
nothing fights or trains; for 2 seconds the survivors near an enemy step back toward their Nexus, for 6 seconds each
side forms up in ranks across its march line (melee in front, the Commander in the middle, ranged behind), and the last
2 seconds let the slowest arrive. That plays on the Build Phase map while the player builds. A building placed where
units stand, friendly or enemy, sends them walking to the nearest free ground, pushing others on. Recall and the walk
home are gone, from the campaign too.
