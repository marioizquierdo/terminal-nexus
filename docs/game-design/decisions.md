# Terminal Nexus — settled product decisions

_Decisions the game depends on, kept as a flat list until each is moved into the design document that owns it as a RULE._

- Title: **Terminal Nexus**.
- Product: deterministic terminal strategy; an LLM is not required.
- Match rhythm: 5–12 minutes.
- Phases: hidden, simultaneous, untimed **Build Phase** followed by a fixed-tick **Nexus Pulse**.
- Resolution speed: presentation is independent from deterministic logical time.
- Early engine/content stack: TypeScript-first; runtime and first terminal backend are evidence-selected.
- Renderer boundary: simulation emits visibility-filtered semantic views and events; the terminal compositor emits an engine-owned cell frame; platform backends are adapters.
- Presentation baseline: monochrome seven-bit ASCII, enhanced by explicit ANSI color and optional Unicode modes.
- The play surface is the **Grid**. The replica on it is a **Grid Nexus**; the one that stays home is a **Prime Nexus**.
- Grid presets: the **default `medium-extra-wide` (48 × 16) is locked**, because both compositions are derived from it. The rest of the preset matrix — sizes `small`/`medium`/`large`/`extra-large` against shapes `squared`/`wide`/`extra-wide` — is GUIDANCE in [`engine.md`](../system-design/grid-engine.md) Section 3.1 and may change when content shows a better set.
- Grid orientation is a rendering choice. Portrait and landscape change nothing about state, rules, or coordinates.
- The Grid has five layers — terrain, obstacles, workers, units, air. **Layers define render order only.** Collision is a mask composed from a chosen set of layers, so cross-layer collision is normal and same-tile coexistence is a property of the masks involved.
- Every entity has an anchor, a footprint, and a facing. **Units as well as structures may span multiple tiles**; a mover tests its whole footprint. Facing is presentation-only until a milestone earns otherwise.
- The viewport is clamped to between 48 × 16 and 72 × 24 tiles, so no display shows meaningfully more Grid than another. The cursor drives scrolling at a 3-tile margin. There is no minimap. The clamp and the cursor-driven rule are locked; **the tuning numbers inside them — the 3-tile margin above all — are locked direction, and Milestone 5 may retune them on evidence** from the first person who actually scrolls a Grid. **That person has now scrolled one** (gate 5A, 2026-09-21): three tiles stands, with Mario's fuller judgement deferred and `--scroll-margin` kept on the command line so another number can still be felt against it. (This line said Milestone 3 until it was corrected the same day: scrolling moved to the Build Phase milestone when the build order was re-cut at canon 2.11, and the old Milestone 3 that owned it — a builder and battle editor — was retired at 2.9.) **That fuller judgement arrived 2026-09-26**: a flat 3-tile margin and a fixed one-tile-per-key-event cursor speed are being retuned into a real speed-tier model with a percentage-of-viewport margin — the mechanism is now GUIDANCE in `engine.md` 3.3, built at gate 5H with every number a live Debug Mode field, and the exact numbers registered OBSERVABLE as Q54.
- Every engine statement declares its authority: **RULE** or **GUIDANCE**. Descriptive completeness is not authorization.
- Tile width is adaptive presentation: one terminal column per tile at 80 columns, two at 128 or wider. Same tiles, same information; 80×24 is the acceptance target.
- One resource per match. Deposits and salvage both yield it; supply is a separate population cap; Nexus energy is a state readout, not a currency.
- Commander: a prominent persistent frontline `@`, fictionally a Nexus Symbol; death causes one full Build Phase/Pulse cycle of absence before restoration.
- Prime Nexus: remains at its home location and replicates a smaller Grid Nexus; Nexuses do not teleport.
- Production: fixed recipes from buildings, not direct unit purchases.
- Victory: destroy the enemy Grid Nexus.
- First integrated factions: Citizens and Ravels.
- First complete single-player direction: Citizen origin campaign.
- Architecture: deterministic kernel, content, scenario, projection, presentation, adapters, and shell remain separate.
- Terminal library and JavaScript runtime are chosen independently; neither implies the other.
- Corruption effects never occupy the `units` or `structures` bands and never remove the only carrier of a required semantic cue.
- An undecided question lives in the open-questions register with a recommendation, not as a hedge inside a specification.
- Modding: preserve future seams but do not build a loader or stable SDK in early docs/milestones.
- Logical time is **12 ticks per simulation second**, and movement credit is an integer accumulator capped at one step's cost that a blocked step keeps. Earned by Milestone 1; durations are authored in raw ticks, so the rate is expensive to change from Milestone 12 onward.
- The seeded gameplay PRNG is **PCG32** (`pcg_setseq_64_xsh_rr_32`), checked against published vectors. Cosmetic randomness is a **hash of an effect instance's identity**, never a stream — a stream's answers depend on how many times it was asked, which is exactly what effect purity forbids.
- A **Grid Nexus is a flag on a content definition**, never a content id the kernel recognises.
- The **compositor enforces the corruption law**: an effect cell that would replace an entity's glyph is dropped, and the only write permitted onto an occupied cell is a glyphless attribute change.
- Faction visual identity lives in the **glyph family and the effect language**. Ownership keeps the colour, so a mirror match stays legible and monochrome stays whole.
- Fixture armies on the bench are **not Commander Armies** and carry no balance claim. Milestone 12 still selects the real Citizens-versus-Ravels microgame.
- `CellStyle.fade` (canon 2.8, Q25): a continuous `fgRole`-only scalar, `0` the role's own colour to `1` the theme's background, resolved only at `color256`/`truecolor`. A narrow, recorded departure from `ascii-effects.md` craft rule 7, scoped to `fx.damage.flash` alone — not a general fade-out licence for effects.
- Roadmap sequencing (canon 2.8): **campaign-first, one level at a time**, not a horizontal "complete the kernel" pass. A level pulls in only the systems its own mission needs; the horizontal contract this replaces is preserved as backlog, not deleted, and is pulled in the day a level's own content actually needs a piece of it.
- Input (canon 2.10, `engine.md` 9.7): **every interactive action is a named command, and keyboard, mouse, and a driver are three adapters onto one vocabulary.** Every menu item displays its hotkey and is clickable with identical effect; a hotkey that is not displayed does not exist; the driver can inject raw key and mouse events into the real adapters and read the cell frame back, so an agent can playtest and a test can assert the mapping itself. The bindings inside that are GUIDANCE.
- Commander Army composition (canon 2.10, revised 2.16, `commander-armies.md` 2.1): **a faction is a wide pool; a Commander Army fields a few of them.** Mario's own parts list (2.16): a Nexus and faction, a Commander, starting units and structures, blueprints and a real inspectable tech tree, upgrades, Nexus powers, and Specials. The tech tree is mostly shared across a faction's Commanders; completing a structure unlocks its dependents through the same `unlockStructure` effect a Nexus power produces, gated only by which structures exist, never a second resource. A Special is cast once per match, during a Build Phase the player chooses. The match only ever sees an army; an authored army, the Campaign's bonus goals, and Challenge's own progression are three producers of the same shape. Caps and roster numbers are GUIDANCE until Milestone 12. **The earlier claim that this composition is "a deck" is retracted outright, not even GUIDANCE** — kept only as an unproven idea pending building and playing (Mario: "the army is more than just a deck... composed of a bunch of different things").
- Naming (canon 2.16, `commander-armies.md` 2.1a): **a generic word may be loose in the interface; a mechanism name must be exact in the model.** Mario: "Card is more from the UI point of view, we can use it as long as the inner term is clear. The same for Upgrade, there can be many types of upgrades... The important terms are not those, but the game concepts without ambiguity." So "card" and "upgrade" stay broad presentation words, while **Nexus Power**, **tech tree**, **blueprint**, **Special**, and **research** each name exactly one mechanism, always. The same split the engine already enforces where cells carry style roles and never literal colors. Prefer a term a strategy or deckbuilder player already knows; put the flavor in an instance's name, never in the category word.
- Nexus draft skip (canon 2.16, `commander-armies.md` 4.5): **a dealt Nexus power may not be declined**, in general — it is almost always strictly advantageous, unlike a typical deckbuilder's rares, so there is nothing to protect a build against by skipping one. Alder alone converts a power into "honor," their own mechanic, and even that may be locked out at tutorial difficulty.
- Campaign/Challenge relationship (canon 2.16, `game-modes.md` 3.2): **the two modes are uncorrelated by design.** Challenge ships with basic Commander packages and unlocks the rest of the faction pools through its own play, independent of the Campaign. The Campaign's bonus goals add a few more unlocks, only for things Challenge has not already granted — additive, never gating. Opening Challenge before the Campaign shows a dismissible recommendation, never a block.
- Single-player modes (canon 2.11, `game-modes.md`): **two, designed for simultaneously — Campaign is the first-time player experience and the world's canon; Challenge is seeded runs with a draft between battles.** A mode is data over one match loop and one army shape; every card carries `rarity`, `tier`, and `role` so both modes can deal it. The Campaign carries no length or breadth requirement. Multiplayer comes later on the same match. The run's numbers are GUIDANCE until Milestone 11 plays them.
- Nexus naming (canon 2.13, `terminal-nexus-lore.md` 3.1): **each Nexus is named for the faction that holds it** — Citizen Nexus, Ravel Nexus, Feudal Nexus, Glitch Nexus, Alder Nexus — with Prime/Grid appended where the distinction matters. No proper names in canon, code, or interface; a character calling one something else is voice, not terminology.
- Nexus powers (canon 2.13, `commander-armies.md` 4.5, Q42 answered): **a name and one plain line of description**, with no player-facing classification. In code the effect is one of `unlockStructure`, `spawnUnits`, `modifyContent`, `modifyRule`, `modifyCommander`, `reveal` — engineering names the player never sees. `reveal` keeps information something a player spends a pick on rather than something the HUD gives away.
- Missions (canon 2.13, `campaigns.md` 4.3, Q44 answered): **a mission has goals, not a fixed length.** A main goal (usually "destroy the enemy Grid Nexus"; also survive/capture/accumulate shapes) and usually a bonus goal, which is what unlocks Challenge content. A Pulse counter appears only when the goal is about Pulses. Bonus goals are shown in the briefing, never a surprise (canon 2.14, Q48).
- Objectives (canon 2.14, `campaigns.md` 2.2): **a bounded `ObjectiveDefinition` union** — `destroyNexus`, `surviveUntil`, `captureAndHold`, `accumulate`, `keepAlive` — resolved by the scenario/trigger layer, one level above the kernel. The kernel's own victory check (`engine.md` Section 5) never learns about a mission's goal; it stays the fallback for Skirmish and every Challenge battle.
- Lore restraint (canon 2.15, `terminal-nexus-lore.md` 10.6): **the lore is a platform, not a plot.** This is a terminal game with icons — complexity grows through units and powers, never through story; a named character must earn its place by teaching a mechanic; budgets are ceilings; there is **one timeline**; and the setting under-specifies deliberately, because the game is built for players extending it with their own agents. The proof burden is that ANSI characters are exciting and legible, and when those two goals compete the Grid wins.
- The opening campaigns (canon 2.13–2.14): **no upfront Commander-choice screen** — a new player starts Vasse's mission 1 directly, and completing it unlocks Averno and Dob Hunter as two more campaign rows (Q43). The Ravel opening reuses PERIMETER's literal map with roles swapped; no second map is authored (Q47).
- Commanders and Nexuses (canon 2.12, `commander-armies.md` 2.2): **a Commander is the Nexus's signature, not the faction's employee.** Affinity — Native, Estranged, Unsanctioned, Foreign, Dual-bound, Proxy — is fiction plus data over the existing composition model, never a special case; the faction still owns the roster.
- Build order (canon 2.11, `docs/milestones/README.md`): **milestone numbers are identities, not an order**; the experience and UX are built before content — the match UX core, then the two mode shells, then depth, then proof and content iteration.
- Missions (canon 2.10, `campaigns.md` 2.1): **a mission is a sequence of Build Phase / Nexus Pulse cycles driven by triggers** — a condition and a list of actions. Simulation actions execute inside the kernel as validated intents and are part of the hashed inputs; presentation actions never touch state. A Pulse may be scripted and is still a Pulse. Whether the authored surface is data or code is Q39.
