# Terminal Nexus — play strategies

_What duels have taught about playing a Build Phase well, as advice a player can act on. A model player reads this
page in its brief (`npm run duel`, [`duels.md`](../system-design/duels.md)), so each entry is written to it: one thing
to do or not to do, why, and the measurement it comes from. Add an entry when a measured duel or a playtest teaches
something a player could use; change or remove one when a later measurement says otherwise. Never an entry no
measurement or playtest backs._

The owner, 2026-10-21: "Maybe we should have a file for play strategies on the ai playing instructions as we discover
more ways to prepare the build phase."

## How to read an entry

Each entry is advice first, then the evidence in brackets: what was played and what it showed. The numbers are from
scripted players, which build by a rule and never adapt; a player that thinks can do better, and should treat the
advice as where to start, not a law.

## What duels have taught so far

- **Where your buildings stand matters more than how tight your army stands.** A building in the enemy's road gets
  razed before it trains much, and a side whose producers fall stops getting waves. Build the buildings that train
  units behind your Nexus or to its sides, out of the line the enemy marches along; push forward only what you can
  defend. [Duels by the scripted rush and balanced players, 2026-10-21: a rush built as a block in front lost about 4
  buildings a battle, built apart 1.3, and the apart side won 35 of 36.]
- **Spread your producers when the enemy hits areas.** A Ravel bomber bursts in the thickest knot of your units, and a
  Citizen mortar's shell lands there and leaves the ground burning. Buildings set apart send their waves out apart,
  and an army built apart took about a third less of that damage than a block. When the enemy trains neither, a block
  meets them as one and wins a clash of lines. [The formation game, 2026-10-21: four Barracks a side against area
  attacks alone; a block took 120 from two mortars and 372 from four bombers, apart 77 and 240, with the mortar's
  first numbers: its burst now hurts only the enemy, and its fire bites harder for less long.]
- **The first two rounds decide most duels: spend early.** The side ahead after round 2 went on to win 50 of 54
  duels. Credits kept for later are worth less than a wave that fights now. [The full measurement after the
  pathfinding milestone's wrap-up, 2026-10-21, 162 duels; 51 of 54 before it.]
- **Do not count on a round-1 rush to end it.** Your enemy's Nexus shoots: one shot every three seconds for 40, at a
  marksman's reach, enough to drop a trooper or a runner in one hit; and it mends 40 between rounds. A march on an
  unguarded Nexus in round 1 ended one duel of 162. Rush to get ahead, not to win at once. [The same measurement, and
  the one before it.]
- **Holding is a real choice for a defender.** Dob Hunter's army wins mostly when it holds in front of its Nexus and
  lets the enemy come to it, under the Nexus's own shots. [The Commander duels, 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21.]
- **Pick the army for the pass.** Eighteen melee troopers beat a mixed army on open ground and lose to it at a short pass
  by a wide margin: a gap four rows wide, two rows or one tile, and a long corridor. A two-row door lets two strike
  abreast; ranged units fire over it and flyers cross it. If the way between the Nexuses is a short pass, field ranged,
  area and flying units, or hold the door yourself. [The engage scenarios, 12 seeds, 2026-10-29: the melee army left 33,
  54 and 68 points of health behind at four rows, two and one tile.]
